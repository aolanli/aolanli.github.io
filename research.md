<p class="page-intro">
I develop and apply statistical methods for complex biomedical data, with a
particular interest in Bayesian model comparison, longitudinal analysis, and
reproducible collaborative research.
</p>

## Selected research areas

<div class="research-stack">
  <article class="research-panel">
    <p class="section-kicker">BAYESIAN METHODS</p>
    <h3>Model comparison and marginal likelihoods</h3>
    <p>
      My methodological work focuses on marginal-likelihood estimation for
      complex Bayesian models, including Monte Carlo strategies for practical
      model comparison. My dissertation,
      <em>Advancing Marginal Likelihoods for Longitudinal Data Analysis</em>,
      studied marginal-likelihood decomposition for covariance-structure
      assessment in repeated-measures randomized trials.
    </p>
    <p>
      <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/wics.70058">Monte Carlo estimator comparison</a>
      <span aria-hidden="true"> · </span>
      <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syad007">LoRaD</a>
    </p>
  </article>

  <article class="research-panel">
    <p class="section-kicker">LONGITUDINAL &amp; CLINICAL RESEARCH</p>
    <h3>Repeated measures, missing data, and simulation</h3>
    <p>
      I work with longitudinal and multivariate models, covariance structures,
      missing data, and simulation-based evaluation. During an ORISE
      appointment at FDA's Center for Drug Evaluation and Research, I developed
      Bayesian and frequentist simulation workflows for oncology trial-design
      and analysis questions, including imaging-window evaluation, treatment
      switching, and survival-data analysis.
    </p>
  </article>

  <article class="research-panel">
    <p class="section-kicker">BIOMEDICAL DATA SCIENCE</p>
    <h3>Microbiome, multi-omics, and health trajectories</h3>
    <p>
      At Yale, I collaborate on NIH-funded studies of neurodevelopment,
      microbiome science, and symptoms. This work combines longitudinal and
      multivariate modeling with microbiome and multi-omics analysis, causal
      mediation, machine learning, and reproducible statistical reporting.
    </p>
    <p>
      <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines13092102">Multi-omics intervention study</a>
      <span aria-hidden="true"> · </span>
      <a href="https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.18.26353470">Gut-microbiota preprint</a>
    </p>
  </article>
</div>

## Methods and computing

<div class="method-grid">
  <div>
    <h3>Bayesian and simulation methods</h3>
    <p>Bayesian inference, MCMC and Monte Carlo computation, marginal-likelihood estimation, model comparison, and simulation-based evaluation.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Clinical and longitudinal methods</h3>
    <p>Repeated measures, longitudinal and multivariate models, LMM, GLMM, MANOVA, MMRM, covariance structures, missing data, and survival analysis.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Biomedical data methods</h3>
    <p>Microbiome and multi-omics analysis, trajectory analysis, causal mediation, regularization, tree-based machine learning, and high-dimensional methods.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Programming and reproducibility</h3>
    <p>R, Python, SAS, Stan, SQL, R Markdown, Shiny, Git, and HPC/SLURM.</p>
  </div>
</div>
